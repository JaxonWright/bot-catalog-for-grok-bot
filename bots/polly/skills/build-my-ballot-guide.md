# Build my ballot guide

Use this when the user wants suggestions for their ballot, or after "Polly getting started".

## Find the ballot
1. Get the official sample ballot for `voter_address` from the state or county election office. If there isn't one yet, use Ballotpedia's sample ballot lookup and Vote411, tell the user it isn't the official ballot yet, and check again once the official one is out.
2. List every contest, top to bottom: each office with its candidates and their parties, every judicial retention, and every ballot measure with its official title. Note any nonpartisan races.

## Research each contest
Go to each candidate's and each measure's own sources first, as your description says, and use news articles only to find those or to cover what only reporting has. Leave out opinion pieces and editorials.

For each candidate:
- Look up their record and positions on each of the user's `priorities`: votes and bills for incumbents, official platforms and campaign sites, debate and forum answers, questionnaires such as Vote411's and Ballotpedia's, public statements, and endorsements. Use ratings from groups on more than one side when they exist.
- Run the "Red flag check" skill on them.
- Give every candidate in a race the same depth of research. If you can find much less about one of them, say so instead of filling the gap.

For each ballot measure, read the official text and the official fiscal note or summary. Find who supports it and who opposes it.

For judges and other nonpartisan races, use bar association ratings, official records and rulings, and candidate questionnaires.

## Suggest
For each contest, compare the candidates against `priorities` and `dealbreakers`, weighted the way the user weighted them. Then write:
- Your suggestion, and whether it's a strong match, a lean, or too close to call.
- Two or three plain sentences on why, naming the priorities that decided it and where the suggested candidate does not match the user.
- Anything from the "Red flag check" worth knowing, for every candidate it applies to.
- The sources.

If a race hinges on an issue the user hasn't covered, ask about it in one short question and wait before you suggest anything there. If a race is too close to call, say what would tip it, and let the user choose. If a candidate is unopposed, say so and still note any red flags.

Don't suggest a straight-party ticket unless each race on its own comes out that way. If the user's party has a candidate who fits them poorly, say so.

## Deliver as a Page
Read the canvas skill first. Make a new Page titled like "Ballot guide, Nov 3 general election". Never put the address in it. Lay it out like the ballot itself, from top to bottom:
- A short voting plan at the top: the date, where to go, the hours, what to bring, and the official links.
- One card per contest, with each candidate's name and party, your suggestion and how sure you are, the why, any red flags, and the sources.
- Measures show what yes does and what no does before your suggestion.
- A clean "My picks" summary at the bottom that fits on one printed page, with an empty box beside each race so the user can mark their own choice. Tell them to check the official page on whether they can bring notes or a phone into the booth.

Send the user a one-line chat note with the Page link and the races that were too close to call. Then ask whether they want to change any priorities or dig into a race.
