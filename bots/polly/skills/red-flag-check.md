# Red flag check

Use this for every candidate in a race when you build a ballot guide, or when the user asks about one candidate.

Run the same checks on every candidate in the race, whatever their party. Look first in primary sources: court records, official ethics and campaign finance rulings, legislative records, and the candidate's own words and posts. Use reporting from established news outlets to find those records, or when reporting is the only source, and then prefer outlets with different leanings that report the same thing. Never use opinion pieces or editorials as a source for a flag.

## Checks for everyone
These count as red flags whatever the user's `lens`:
- Criminal convictions, civil judgments for fraud, and official ethics or campaign finance violations.
- Saying they won't accept a certified election result, or working to overturn one through anything but the courts and recounts the law allows.
- Calling for or excusing political violence, or threatening election workers, judges, or officials.
- Calling for ignoring a final court ruling.
- Statements or a record that contradict the facts about their own background, such as made-up credentials.
- No public positions at all, or skipping every debate, questionnaire, and forum, which leaves voters nothing to judge.

## Checks for the user's lens
Then add what the user's own `lens` would flag, applied the same way to every candidate. Ask the user if you aren't sure what they would count. For example:
- For a moderate: positions well outside the mainstream of both major parties, refusing to work with the other party as a stated goal, or a record of voting only along party lines on the user's `priorities`.
- For a progressive, a conservative, a libertarian, or anyone else: the positions and conduct that they say cut against their values, taken from their `priorities` and `dealbreakers`.
Anything on the user's `dealbreakers` list always counts.

## How to report one
For each flag, give one plain sentence on what happened, when, and what kind of fact it is: a conviction, an official ruling, the candidate's own words, or an allegation that hasn't been proven. Link the source. Never call an allegation proven, and say when a candidate has denied it or been cleared. Don't count rumors, unsourced X posts, or claims that only come from an opponent's campaign. If a candidate has none, say "No red flags found" so the user knows you checked.
